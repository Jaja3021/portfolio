When you start looking for a house and lot or condo, one of the first questions is: **ready-for-occupancy (RFO) or pre-selling?** Both can be a good choice. It depends on when you need to move in, how much cash you have now, and how much waiting you can live with.

## What do RFO and pre-selling mean?

- **RFO (Ready for Occupancy):** the unit is finished. You can inspect the actual house or condo and move in soon after your loan is approved.
- **Pre-selling:** the developer sells units before or during construction. You reserve now and move in when the project is completed, often 2 to 5 years later.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | RFO | Pre-selling |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Higher, close to market value | Lower, usually the developer's best price |
| **Down payment / equity** | Shorter term, often 6 to 12 months, or spot cash | Longer and lighter, often spread over 12 to 48 months |
| **Move-in** | Within months | After construction, usually years |
| **What you see** | The actual unit | A model house, floor plan, or rendering |
| **Monthly amortization** | Starts soon after move-in | Starts after turnover |
| **Risk** | Low, the unit already exists | Possible construction delays or changes |
| **Rent savings** | Stop paying rent sooner | Keep paying rent while waiting |

## When RFO is the better choice

- You are **renting now** and want to stop paying rent as soon as possible.
- You need to **move in by a specific date**, for example before school starts or before your family arrives.
- You want to **inspect the exact unit**, including its finishing, orientation, and neighbors, before paying.
- You can afford a **bigger down payment** over a shorter period.

## When pre-selling is the better choice

- You want the **lowest price** and the lightest monthly equity.
- You are **not in a rush** to move in. This is common for OFWs planning to come home in a few years.
- You are buying as an **investment** and expect the value to rise by turnover.
- You want the **best unit choices**, such as corner lots or units with the best view.

<Callout>
  Before reserving a pre-selling unit, ask for the developer's **DHSUD License to Sell (LTS)**. It shows that the project is registered and allowed to sell to the public. I include the LTS number on my listings whenever it's available.
</Callout>

## Questions to ask before you decide

1. When do I need to move in, really?
2. How much can I pay monthly for the equity, and for the loan afterwards?
3. For pre-selling: what is the target turnover date, and what is the developer's track record?
4. For RFO: is the price still negotiable, and are there move-in promos?
