The **Pag-IBIG Fund housing loan** is one of the most affordable ways for Filipino workers, including OFWs, to buy a house and lot or condo. It usually has lower interest rates and longer terms than bank loans. Here's what you need to know before applying.

<Callout type="note">
  Pag-IBIG updates its rules, rates, and forms from time to time. Use this guide as a checklist, and confirm the current requirements with Pag-IBIG Fund or with me before you submit.
</Callout>

## Who can apply?

You may qualify for a Pag-IBIG housing loan if you:

- Are an **active Pag-IBIG member** with at least **24 monthly contributions**. If you're short, you can usually pay the remaining months in a lump sum.
- Are **not more than 65 years old** when you apply.
- Have **no Pag-IBIG housing loan that was foreclosed, cancelled, or bought back** due to default.
- Have the **legal capacity to buy property**, and pass Pag-IBIG's background and credit checks.

## How much can you borrow?

- Up to **₱6,000,000**, depending on your capacity to pay, the appraised value of the property, and your age.
- Loan terms of up to **30 years**.
- The interest rate depends on the **repricing period** you choose. Shorter fixed periods usually come with lower rates.

You can estimate your monthly amortization with the [Pag-IBIG tab of my calculator](/calculator).

## Documents you'll need

### For all borrowers
- Housing Loan Application form, with a recent ID photo
- Two valid government-issued IDs (yours, and your spouse's if married)
- Pag-IBIG MID number
- Proof of income (see below)
- PSA marriage certificate, if married

### Proof of income
- **Locally employed:** Certificate of Employment and Compensation, latest payslips, or your latest Income Tax Return
- **Self-employed:** Income Tax Return with audited financial statements, DTI or business permit, and bank statements
- **OFWs:** employment contract or Certificate of Employment and Compensation, plus payslips or remittance records

### If someone will process the loan for you (common for OFWs)
- **Special Power of Attorney (SPA)**, apostilled or consularized if signed abroad
- Valid IDs of your attorney-in-fact

### Property documents
For a new house and lot or condo, the **developer usually prepares the property documents**, such as the title, tax declaration, and contract to sell. For a pre-owned property, the seller provides them.

## Step-by-step process

1. **Choose the property** and pay the reservation fee to the developer.
2. **Pay the equity / down payment** based on the developer's payment terms.
3. **Submit your loan documents.** The developer or I will help you check that they're complete.
4. **Pag-IBIG evaluates** your application and appraises the property.
5. **Loan approval**, after which you sign the loan documents.
6. **Loan take-out**: Pag-IBIG releases the loan proceeds to the developer, and you start paying your monthly amortization to Pag-IBIG.

<Callout>
  **Tip:** Check your contributions and loan eligibility online through **Virtual Pag-IBIG** before you reserve. It helps you avoid surprises later.
</Callout>

## Common reasons for delays

- Missing or **unsigned** documents, or IDs that are expired
- **Gaps in contributions** that haven't been paid
- Proof of income that doesn't match the declared salary
- For OFWs: an SPA that is **not apostilled or consularized**, or that doesn't specifically cover the loan
